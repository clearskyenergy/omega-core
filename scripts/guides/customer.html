Your account on your phone

Your company's account with your supplier: site plans in Editor Lite, the account's orders with their milestone and warranty, purchase orders, and Fleet — your sites, where each unit is going, when it arrived, which site it runs at. Everyone at your company on the account sees the same.

The app wears your supplier's name and icon. What you do in it — a purchase order, a question on an order, where a battery is going — is what your supplier sees the moment it is saved.

2. Put it on your phone and sign in

It is a web app: nothing to download from a store, and every update reaches the phone the next time it is opened. Open the link your supplier sent once; the app remembers your supplier after that.

iPhone or iPad (Safari)

  1. Open Safari and go to the link.
  2. Tap ••• at the end of the address bar, then Share (see a Share button, the square with the arrow? Tap it directly).
  3. Scroll the sheet and tap Add to Home Screen, then Add.
  4. Open it from the Home Screen from now on, and sign in there: it keeps its own sign-in, separate from Safari.

Android (Chrome)

  1. Open Chrome and go to the link.
  2. Tap the Chrome menu (⋮) and choose Install app or Add to Home screen.
  3. Confirm. The app appears with your other apps, with its own icon. Open it and sign in.

Signing in

  • In the app on an iPhone Home Screen (the picture): Continue with Google, or your work email and a password. There is no emailed link there: it would open in Safari, not in the app.
  • New here? Tap First time here? Create a password, type your work email and a password (6 characters or more). Open the link we email to confirm the address (it may open in Safari; that is fine), then come back.
  • Signed in before with an emailed link, and no password? Type your email and tap Forgot password? to set one.
  • In a browser, or on Android: also Email me a sign-in link; open the email on the same phone (not there in a minute? Look in spam or junk).

3. Home and the five tabs

Along the bottom: Home · Orders · POs · Fleet · Account.

  • Home: your account at a glance. Fleet in the middle, with Size, Design, Warranty, POs, Shipping and Pay around it; a red number is where you are needed. Tap one to open it (each has ‹ Home to come back). Under the hub: what is arriving and what there is to pay.
    • Size and Design: a quick size (kW and hours against your supplier's catalog) with Lay it out and Send as a PO; Site Map · Editor Lite for a site plan from an address, and every saved plan.
    • Pay: open invoices with your supplier's pay link. Shipping: loads on the way and delivered. Warranty: every unit's coverage.
  • Orders: each order with its milestone (received, confirmed, building, testing, ready, shipped), lines with the warranty date, invoices with the pay link, destinations and loads, and Request a change or ask a question.
  • POs: send purchase orders (section 4) and follow the ones under review.
  • Fleet: your sites and every unit on its way to them or running at them (section 5).
  • Account: your account number, company, rep and terms; People on this account (the owner adds colleagues, approves anyone who asked to join, and turns access off for someone who left); your details, documents and agreements.

Your company's account. Signing in from your company email asks to join your company's account with this supplier: its owner (or your supplier) approves you, then you see everything on it. With no account yet, the first sign-in opens one and you are its owner.

4. Purchase orders, straight from a spreadsheet

  1. Tap POs. One line per product per PO, in this order: PO number, SKU, qty, ship-to name, address, city, state, ZIP, requested date, notes. The products you can order are listed under the box.
  2. Copy the rows from Excel or Google Sheets and paste them: columns and a header row are understood (the header is skipped). Or type the lines with commas; an address with a comma in it goes in "double quotes".
  3. Read the line under the box: how many POs and lines it read, or what to fix and on which line.
  4. Tap Send these purchase orders. Each is received for pricing; nothing is charged.

One PO number is one order to one place. A PO going to several sites goes through One PO to several sites; a PO number with two different addresses is not sent until you fix it. A ZIP your spreadsheet shortened (2134 for 02134) gets its zero back where ZIPs start with 0.

5. Fleet: your sites and your units

Tap Fleet (the page is headed Sites & equipment). Your sites, then Your units: every unit shipped on your orders, with where it is and its warranty.

  1. Add a site: where the units will be interconnected — name, address, utility, meter, point of interconnection. Once. Many at once: Sites from a list (below).
  2. While a unit is in transit, choose its site under Going to… and tap Set. When it arrives, set Arrived on (today unless you change it) and its condition, and tap Received: it is placed at that site.
  3. Or tap Received first, then choose the site under Installed at… and tap Assign. A unit already at a site shows that site, and the button reads Move.
  4. Set Commissioned on and who did it, and tap Commissioned.
  5. Each unit shows its warranty (from, until) and any service level. The warranty is tied to the site. Until your supplier confirms your placement it reads awaiting your supplier's confirmation; then confirmed by your supplier with the date.

The app asks before it records a date, and a date can't be in the future or before the unit shipped or arrived: that date starts the warranty. What happened shows on the unit's own card.

Sites from a list — many sites at once

Your PO covers many sites and you have the addresses in an email or a spreadsheet? Fleet → Sites from a list, three steps:

  • 1 Paste the list as it is: one address per line, Street, City, ST ZIP. Bullets and numbering are fine; greetings and sign-offs are left out. To name a site, put the name first with a colon (North store: 2200 Lake Ave, …); to set its units, a count at the end (× 4 or (3 units)). Or upload the file (.csv, .tsv or .txt; from Excel: Save As → CSV). Tap Preview the sites.
  • 2 Check: one card per line, marked new or already yours. Fix a name, untick a line that is not a site (a signature starts unticked). Tap Create … sites: only the new ones are made.
  • 3 Send: choose the order, tick the sites it goes to and type how many units each takes (a blank takes an even share). Preview lists the serials for each site. Send … units to … sites is the confirmation: nothing else will ask. Each unit is then going to its site, and receiving it places it there. Download CSV gives you one row per unit with its site's address.

6. How it reaches your supplier

  1. A PO anyone on the account sends from the app lands with your supplier as an order to price. Everyone on the account sees it under Orders once it is confirmed.
  2. Your supplier accepts and invoices; the milestone on your order moves as their plant scans your units — nobody types a status.
  3. When your units ship, each one appears under Fleet with its serial. You say where it is going and when it arrived; you assign it to the site it runs at; you record commissioning.
  4. Your supplier sees exactly what you said and confirms it. From then on the unit's warranty and any service level are on record against that site, and a replacement under RMA carries the remaining term.
  5. A question, a delivery change or a warranty claim goes in under the order (Request a change or ask a question); the answer comes back on the same order.
One account, one record. The app, the desktop portal and your supplier all read the same record, and so does everyone on your company's account. Nothing is stored only on the phone.

7. If something looks wrong

  • "This link is missing its supplier": open the app from the link your supplier sent once; the app remembers the supplier after that.
  • No emailed link in the app on an iPhone: that is on purpose (it would open in Safari). Use Google, or your email and a password: First time here? Create a password, or Forgot password? if you have signed in before.
  • "Check your inbox" but nothing came: wait a minute and look in spam or junk; the email comes from a no-reply address.
  • The app looks stale: close it fully and open it again. Updates arrive on the next open.
  • Offline: the app says so; reconnect and try again.
  • Questions: your supplier — your account rep is on the Account tab.
  • "Your request to join … is waiting for approval": your company already has an account; its owner or your supplier lets you in.
  • "Open your customer account first": tap Account once, or ask your company's account owner to add you.
  • A serial is "not on one of your orders": the unit was shipped on an order for a different company account. Ask your supplier which account it is on.
  • A date is refused: it is in the future, or before the unit shipped or arrived. If the date your supplier recorded is wrong, ask them to correct it.
  • Editor Lite on a phone: it opens in the browser at desktop width; it lays out best on a tablet or a desktop.
