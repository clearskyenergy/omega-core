What they do

Pull the trigger on a unit label. If the unit is not where the bench expects it, the tablet says so in a sentence the operator can act on.

What the system does

A scan can only advance one station or be a duplicate. It can never skip, reverse, release a hold, close a non-conformance or mark anything shipped — so the worst a stolen scanner achieves is marking units present at one bench, in order. Two stations are written by the test rig and refuse a human scan outright. The station comes off the paired tablet, never off the scan, which is only a string a stranger could type.

The one gap worth naming out loud

Step 9 is the only hand-off in the chain a person still makes by hand. Everything either side of it already runs on its own — the order arrives without anyone rekeying it, and the ship date moves without anyone updating it. Closing that one gap is what turns thirteen steps into one continuous system, and it is the first thing to build next.
