Two flows, one project. Flow 1 is our business process, from site intake and screening through the customer proposal and handoff. Flow 2 is step 4 of it: how the value stack is built, from site and utility inputs through eligible programs, the forecast and dispatch assumptions to portfolio reporting.

Today the number a customer sees rests on the inputs, a regional forecast and a ten-year cash flow. Program eligibility, dispatch and the check that revenue streams can be combined are not yet applied to it. That is where DividendVPP and Lightsmith come in, and where we need to agree who does what.

Who checks that revenue streams can actually be combined?

Today, no one. OMEGA's revenue model adds the streams, and no program's rules on combining them are held anywhere in OMEGA.

On the sample site, PJM capacity ($69,989) and the utility VPP ($150,000) would both count the same 1 MW in the same summer peak hours, and managing the demand charge needs the battery at the monthly peak as well.

Whether the same kW can be paid twice is a program rule, not a modeling choice. The answer moves this site's stack by $70k to $150k a year.

  1. The market participant of record, the aggregator or program operator, owns each program's combination rules, because the program holds them to it.
  2. Lightsmith's optimizer enforces the rules hour by hour, choosing one program for an hour where two can't combine.
  3. OMEGA applies the rules before a stack reaches a proposal: a stream the rules exclude isn't added, and the proposal says which combination it assumes.

Proposed: the participant of record owns the rules, Lightsmith enforces them in dispatch, and OMEGA applies them in the stack. To decide in the meeting: the named owner, and where the rules table lives.
