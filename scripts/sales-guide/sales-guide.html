The pitch
Thirty seconds, then the rules of the ladder
Say: "Everything you pay separate people and separate tools for today, from screening a site to getting it built, in one workspace. Start at %%LITE_PRICE%% a month. Add only the parts you actually use, when you use them. Nothing is per seat: one person designs, twenty can look."

What they do in the first week

1

Screen a site. Type an address. Omega Grid shows the substations, lines and hosting capacity around it; Omega Intel scores the parcel, terrain and network before anyone drives there.

2

Design it. Omega Design draws the site on live satellite: equipment, conduit, a guided build for BESS, solar, EV or a microgrid, and a blueprint and proposal for the customer the same day.

3

Price it and prove it. Omega Estimate turns the design into an electrical estimate, a bill of materials and a request for quote; Omega Storage sizes the battery and prints the pro forma.

4

Keep going. Plan sets for the utility and the AHJ, the permitting matrix, financing, then operations, and Omega Logic when they build and ship product of their own.

Why it saves on project development

Every module replaces something the customer buys today: a data subscription, a CAD seat, an estimating seat, a modelling tool, consultant or drafting hours. The menu on the next page names the typical figure beside each module so the rep can put the customer's own number next to ours.

The other saving is time. A site that used to take a week of handoffs between the screener, the designer and the estimator is one person's afternoon, and the proposal goes out while the customer is still interested.

How a customer opts in
The guided signup, the Modules page inside the workspace, and the rep's four moves

Signing up: a guided run

1

Account. Work email only (a public address is refused); Google or a password. Create account lands straight in the run.

2

What does your team do? The twelve tiles, or Skip. The dock prices the recommendation as they tap.

3

Your system. The one menu, priced live: monthly, or yearly at %%ANNUAL_MONTHS%% months of twelve, with the first-year service fee named.

4

Confirm email (only while unconfirmed), then Billing & pay: the short billing form, then the invoice's card page.

5

In. Paid means open, within minutes, on the package they chose.

A link from the price list carries the package (?modules= or ?plan=) and skips the question screen; so does a proposal link. The draft is kept in their browser, so the email link reopening the page resumes where they were.

Inside the workspace: the Modules page

Every module is listed for every workspace, each card with one status and at most one action:

  • Live, or Live, always included (Omega Design).
  • Not on your plan with Opt in: the menu states the money first, then Opt in and pay (or Turn it on for a $0 addition inside a plan's cap). Paid, the plan re-reads itself and the tools open without a reload.
  • Waiting for payment with Pay, I've paid and Cancel request.
  • Opt out queues the removal for the quarterly review and states the fee before and after; Cancel request withdraws it.

Bought is visible. Inside Site Map, a ribbon tab the plan stops at is never blank: it reads Opt in with the module and the price, and a locked tool tile names the module that carries it. A member who cannot buy is told who to ask. Showing it is never access: the commands stay shut until it is paid.

Legacy workspaces (billed by ClearSky outside the engine) opt in the same way: by card as an add-on beside their plan where the module switches on exactly, else as a recorded request ClearSky activates.

The rep's four moves

1

Demo from the price list. Screen an address, draw it, price it, in front of them. Twenty minutes. Then tap the tiles together.

2

Send the Subscription Proposal. From the discovery, branded for their company, the value page with their numbers, the order form. Accepting it prices nothing; signup does.

3

Follow the first month. Signed up but never signed in, signed in but nothing built, payment overdue: the growth board flags each, with the next action. Nothing built in the first two weeks is the call.

4

Flip them up. When they hit a tab that says Opt in, that is the next sale, and they can make it themselves. Watch what they build; propose the module that finishes it.

Say it straight
Objections, and the limits an honest rep names before the customer finds them

Objections

"That's a lot per month." Compared with what? Put their spend on tools, data and hours next to the modules they picked; the proposal does it line by line. Start smaller: Omega Design alone is %%LITE_PRICE%%, and every module is one click later.

"We already have CAD / a modelling tool / a GIS person." Keep them. Buy the modules that replace what you do not have, and skip the ones that duplicate what you do. Nothing forces a bundle.

"How many licences do we need?" None. A workspace comes with %%BUILDERS%% builders and %%VIEWERS%% viewers, and the whole company works in it.

"Can we try it first?" The demo is the try: screen, draw and price one of their own sites with them on the call. Then start on Omega Design alone at %%LITE_PRICE%% a month; there is no trial, and there is nothing to lose by starting small, because every module is one click later and comes off at the quarterly review.

"What if we stop needing a module?" Opt out from the Modules page; it comes off at the quarterly review and the price of what stays is stated before you confirm.

"Is our data safe?" Every workspace is its own tenant; access is enforced on the server, not hidden in the page. Pricing, sizing and scoring run on our servers, never in the browser.

"Can it wear our brand?" Omega Storefront: your name on the platform, sizing on your own website, leads and orders into your queue. It needs the reseller addendum.
